Last time I called *Midnight Marauders* another formative favorite. It's the project with the most fully developed view of the A Tribe Called Quest sonic palette. I had many songs from this record on repeat, but didn't fully appreciate the complete project until later. Now, it's one of my favorite albums to listen to on vinyl.

## The sound

The sound is clear but still hits hard. It's not as clean as *The Low End Theory*, but that's on purpose. This is about as close to collage-style production as Tribe would ever get, mixing in different vocal snippets and disembodied narrators to usher the album's flow along. Underrated piece -- Ali Shaheed's cutting / scratching. Interlude type beats galore.

## More voices

There are various perspectives and storytellers present throughout the album, which is a welcome feature after the reliance on Q-Tip for MC duties on the first two albums. Phife Dawg steps up in a big way to fill those shoes, and on *Midnight Marauders* Q-Tip is able to focus more on production and songwriting. It's their best work for exactly that reason.

## Every song holds up

"Steve Biko (Stir It Up)," "Award Tour," and of course "Electric Relaxation" are the standout tracks, but literally every song holds up. "Electric Relaxation" by itself has thousands of plays from me. To me, this is what pushes it past *The Low End Theory* -- its completeness as a project.

## Ahead of its time

In some ways I think this album perfected the style many groups were chasing in the mid 90s, but Tribe is releasing this in 1993 (!) whereas many other artists would only get here by '95 or '96. Slum Village and J Dilla were definitely heavily influenced by the album and the way it was mixed. *Midnight Marauders* is probably one of the most influential full hip-hop albums of the era.
